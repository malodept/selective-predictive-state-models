Public PushT exact-reset audit. The public logged PushT bridge shows that logged transitions alone are insufficient to prove action-conditioning: action ablations preserve much of the ranking signal. We therefore instantiate the audit in `gym_pusht/PushT-v0`, where exact simulator resets are available. For each initial state, we replay five action targets from the same state and rank the correct future among same-state action futures.

In state space, clean-action prediction reaches 0.971/0.989/0.988 for Tiny/Medium/Full at K=5, while zero-action, shuffled-action, random-action, shuffled-prediction, and zero-delta controls remain near chance. Because PushT actions are target positions for the agent, we additionally train a block-only predictor whose output is restricted to block displacement. This object-only predictor remains far above chance and capacity-ordered, reaching 0.318/0.544/0.609 for Tiny/Medium/Full, versus 0.200 chance, while all controls remain near chance.

The visual audit reveals an additional representation-alignment issue. Global DINO deltas are only weakly predictable, but cropping around the block makes the visual target substantially more predictable. However, with block-centered crops, using actions expressed relative to the agent remains weak. Re-expressing the action as an absolute target improves the visual ranking signal, and expressing it relative to the block is strongest: crop-DINO ranking reaches 0.659±0.020 at K=5, versus 0.200 chance, while shuffled-action and shuffled-prediction controls remain near chance. Thus exact-reset evaluation recovers a genuine public action-conditioned counterfactual signal and diagnoses whether state and action representations are geometrically aligned.
